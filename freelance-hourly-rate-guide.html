How to Calculate Your Freelance Hourly Rate

A simple guide to setting an hourly rate that reflects your income goals, expenses, and actual billable time.

A simple freelance hourly-rate formula

A good starting point is to consider both the income you want to earn and the costs of running your freelance business.

Hourly Rate = (Desired Annual Income + Annual Business Expenses) ÷ Annual Billable Hours
Example:

If your desired annual income is $50,000, your annual business expenses are $5,000, and you expect 1,440 billable hours:

($50,000 + $5,000) ÷ 1,440 = $38.19/hour

This gives you a useful starting point. Your final rate can then be adjusted according to your experience, skills, specialization, market, and the type of clients you serve.

Remember: not every working hour is billable

Freelancers spend time on many activities that cannot always be charged directly to a client, such as:

  • Client communication and emails
  • Proposals and meetings
  • Finding new clients
  • Marketing and administration
  • Bookkeeping and business management
  • Learning and professional development

For this reason, your total working hours will usually be higher than your billable hours. Using a realistic billable-hour estimate helps prevent underpricing your services.

Don't forget your business expenses

Your freelance rate should also leave room for the costs of running your business. Depending on your work, these may include:

  • Software and online subscriptions
  • Internet and equipment
  • Payment or platform fees
  • Marketing expenses
  • Professional services
  • Taxes and other business costs

So, what should you charge?

Your calculated hourly rate is a starting point, not a fixed rule. Your experience, expertise, demand, project complexity, client type, and market can all influence what you can reasonably charge.

The goal isn't simply to offer the lowest price. Your rate should make your freelance work sustainable, professional, and worthwhile.
